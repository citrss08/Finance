Sources of finance

Businesses need finance to start up, run day to day (working capital) and grow (capital expenditure).

Internal

  • Retained profit: profit reinvested. Cheap, but limited by profit.
  • Sale of assets: raises cash quickly, but the asset is lost.
  • Personal funds: owner's savings.

External

  • Share capital: sell shares. No repayment, but ownership is diluted.
  • Loan capital: borrow and repay with interest.
  • Overdraft: short-term, flexible, high interest.
  • Trade credit, leasing, crowdfunding, venture capital.

Short-term needs (stock, wages) suit short-term finance. Long-term needs (buildings, machinery) suit long-term finance.

Costs and revenue

Total cost (TC) = FC + VC
Total revenue (TR) = P × Q
Profit = TR − TC
Average cost = TC ÷ Q

Fixed costs

Do not change with output in the short run: rent, salaries, insurance.

Variable costs

Change directly with output: raw materials, packaging, piece-rate pay.

Direct costs link to a specific product. Indirect (overhead) costs cannot easily be traced to one product.

Break-even analysis

The break-even point is the output where total revenue equals total cost, so profit is zero. It is the crossing point in the chart above.

Contribution per unit = P − VC
Break-even output = FC ÷ (P − VC)
Margin of safety = actual output − break-even output
Target output = (FC + target profit) ÷ (P − VC)
Worked example

P = $20, VC = $8, FC = $60,000. Contribution = 20 − 8 = $12. Break-even = 60,000 ÷ 12 = 5,000 units. If the business sells 6,500 units, the margin of safety is 1,500 units. For a $24,000 profit: (60,000 + 24,000) ÷ 12 = 7,000 units.

Strengths: simple, helps with planning and loans. Limitations: assumes straight lines, all output is sold, and costs and prices stay constant.

Ratio analysis

Gross profit margin = gross profit ÷ revenue × 100
Net profit margin = net profit ÷ revenue × 100
ROCE = operating profit ÷ capital employed × 100
Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities
Acid test = (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities
Worked example (using the accounts above)

Gross margin = 200 ÷ 500 = 40%. Net margin = 50 ÷ 500 = 10%. ROCE = 60 ÷ 400 = 15% (capital employed = equity + non-current liabilities). Current assets = 120, current liabilities = 60, so current ratio = 2.0. Acid test = (120 − 40) ÷ 60 = 1.33.

Profitability ratios compare profit with revenue or capital. Liquidity ratios show if short-term debts can be paid. A current ratio of about 1.5 to 2 and an acid test near 1 are often seen as healthy, but it depends on the industry. Compare with past years and competitors.

Investment appraisal

Used to decide whether a project is worth the money. Example: invest $100,000, with returns of $30,000, $40,000, $50,000 and $40,000 over 4 years.

Payback = time to recover the initial investment
ARR = average annual profit ÷ initial investment × 100
NPV = sum of discounted cash flows − initial investment

Payback

After year 2 you have recovered 70,000. Left: 30,000 of year 3's 50,000. So 2 + 30 ÷ 50 = 2.6 years (about 2 years 7 months).

ARR

Total returns = 160,000. Profit = 60,000. Average = 15,000 per year. 15,000 ÷ 100,000 = 15%.
